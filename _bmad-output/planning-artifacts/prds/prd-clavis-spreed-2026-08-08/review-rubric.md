# PRD Quality Review — Clavis Talk: Thread Management and Navigation

## Overall verdict

This is a strong PRD carried by unusual discipline in two places: FRs that each enumerate testable consequences rather than gesturing at behaviour, and a cost section (§6.3) that names what was given up instead of claiming a balance. The personas are inferred, and they earn their place anyway — several requirements exist *because* of a journey and say so. What is at risk is narrower than the document's polish suggests: FR-26 and FR-27 contain consequences that cannot both pass, deferred to architecture in a way that will produce contradictory acceptance criteria downstream; the ten issue items are genuinely specified with two exceptions (the pin bound has no number, and item 8's user-visible payoff ships in a later epic while the traceability table reads as covered); and the research that fixed vocabulary looked at four competitors while missing that "tag", "pin" and "archive" are already shipped user-facing words in this exact build.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions and their costs are named. §6.3 "Cost of the Chosen Unread Model" is the clearest example in the document: per-Thread unread independence is chosen, and three costs — write volume, permanent fork divergence on a table upstream reversed itself on, and "a split notion of 'read'" — are accepted in the open rather than smoothed. §6.1 does the same for merge liability, calling the read-marker reinstatement "the largest single merge liability in this work." §8's non-goal "Not a moderated taxonomy" volunteers that the choice "makes this product an outlier: of the four comparable tools researched, only Discord has per-thread labels and they are predefined." A reader pushing back on free-text labels finds their objection already written down, with the mitigation (FR-25 suggestion) and the escape hatch (v2 label administration) named.

The Open Questions are genuinely open. Each of the eight carries an owner, and three carry an explicit blocking status — Q5 "Resolve before the epics workflow", Q8 "Blocks FR-31 and FR-35", Q1 "must be settled there, not discovered in code." That is more than most PRDs manage. Q2 is unusual and to the document's credit: it flags its own weakest assumption (reactions as writes) and §16 item 6 repeats the self-criticism verbatim — "Weakest assumption in the document."

Where decision-readiness slips is at a single point that reads as decided and is not. FR-21 requires "at most a fixed small number of Pinned Threads", its consequence says "The limit is a server-side constant", and the assumption says "a hard bound around ten." No number is set anywhere, and unlike the label bounds — which give exact figures with a cited precedent — this one is not raised as an Open Question either. It sits in the one gap between decided and open.

### Findings

- **high** Pin bound is presented as settled but has no value (§4.5 FR-21, §16 item 9) — FR-21's consequences require an error "naming the limit" and the limit "stated in the interface", both of which need a number the PRD never supplies. `[ASSUMPTION: a hard bound around ten.]` is not implementable, and unlike FR-22's five-and-twenty it has no cited precedent to resolve it. It also appears in no Open Question, so nothing downstream is obliged to decide it. *Fix:* set the number in FR-21 (ten is fine — Discord's `available_tags` cap of 20 and its pinned-message caps are both nearby precedents), or promote it to Open Question 9 with an owner.
- **medium** §12's naming risk is stale and contradicts §4.1 (§12 bullet 8) — "Mitigation: comparable-product research, **pending at the time of writing**, feeding a naming decision before the interface strings are written." The research is done (addendum §10), the decision is made, and FR-7's Notes state "The requester has confirmed **Ongoing / Closed / Locked**". A reader who reaches §12 first is told the central naming decision is still open. *Fix:* rewrite the risk to what remains live — that a participant arriving from GitHub/Jira will misread Closed — and point the mitigation at FR-7's indicator consequence.
- **medium** §14 traces one direction only, hiding which requirements exceed the issue (§14, §0) — the table maps issue item → FRs, so a decision-maker can confirm nothing was dropped but cannot see what was added. FR-3 — soft-close reopen-by-posting, one of the most consequential behaviours in the document and the source of the FR-3/FR-6 permission asymmetry — appears in no row at all. Its justification is Discord precedent (addendum §10.2), not the requester's ask, and that distinction is invisible. *Fix:* add a "PRD-originated" marker or a short second table for FRs with no issue item behind them, so the requester can see what they are being asked to accept beyond what they filed.

## Substance over theater — strong

The personas are inferred, tagged as inferred (§2 opening, §16 item 1 correctly ranks it "Highest-leverage item in this list"), and they are not theater. Four protagonists across five journeys is under the rubric's ceiling, and each one is traceable to a requirement that would not otherwise exist:

- Hạnh's Friday tidy-up produces FR-13's default-Ongoing filter, FR-2's deliberately cheap close ("a moderator tidying a room does not have to be sure"), and FR-29's mark-as-read-from-the-list, whose assumption is argued from her journey directly — "Without it, tidying a room means opening every Thread, which defeats the Directory."
- Dũng, explicitly "a regular member, not a moderator", is why FR-8's first consequence tests the root-message author path separately from the moderator path.
- Linh's return produces FR-29's first-unread positioning and FR-19's search over the Followed Thread List.
- Every UJ's edge case lands as a specific FR consequence: UJ-1's Saturday reply is FR-3, UJ-3's failed search is FR-18, UJ-4's mid-flight lock is FR-5's composer consequence and FR-34's third, UJ-5's typo is FR-25's second.

That is the test the rubric sets, and the PRD passes it on evidence rather than assertion.

The Vision is specific rather than swappable. "A room with two hundred threads is a room where nobody uses threads, because finding the one you want costs more than starting a new one. The feature defeats itself at exactly the scale it was built for" could not be lifted into another PRD. The NFRs mostly resist boilerplate too — "Scale is the point" names the condition (a thousand Threads, two hundred participants) and refuses to call it a stretch target; the accessibility bullet has a product-specific hook ("which matters specifically for Thread Labels, where colour is the point"); the localisation bullet names Vietnamese and the three places it bites (search matching, truncation, sorting). No differentiation section was invented.

The gap is on the axis that matters commercially. §2 defines the target user as "inside those customer organisations, not Clavis staff", and then §5 and §6.3 load real costs onto a stakeholder who appears in no journey and no metric: the customer's own sysadmin, who applies a migration in place "without a maintenance window" to a table that is "the highest-write-rate table this feature touches", and who is the person who will file the ticket when Thread Read Marker rows outgrow the estimate. For a product sold to self-hosters, that person is at least as close to the purchase decision as Hạnh.

### Findings

- **medium** No persona, journey or metric for the self-hosting operator (§2, §5, §6.3) — the migration NFR, the row-growth NFR and SM-C4 all exist for this stakeholder's benefit, and none of them is connected to a stated need or an acceptance owner. SM-C4 watches row growth "before a customer discovers it does not" — the customer in that sentence is an operator with no representation in §2. *Fix:* add a fifth journey or, more cheaply, a JTBD entry in §2.1 ("Upgrade without a maintenance window and without a capacity surprise") so the §5 migration and growth NFRs have a stated need behind them rather than only an engineering instinct.
- **low** UJ-3 asserts a number it cannot know (§2.3 UJ-3) — "**Climax:** eleven minutes in, she knows what she missed." In a document that is scrupulous elsewhere about not having usage data, an invented duration reads as a smuggled target. *Fix:* cut the figure or make it an explicit illustration.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is real and stated: threads fail at the scale they were built for because the list has no lifecycle and no front door. Features follow from it rather than from a wishlist — the Directory exists because the list has no front door, lifecycle exists because the list cannot distinguish live from dead, and §4.7's long preamble argues from the thesis to reject the cheap unread implementation ("Deriving from it would mean reading one Thread clears the badges on all of them, which is the same uselessness the feature exists to fix"). The scope kind is coherently problem-solving throughout.

The counter-metrics are the strongest part of §10 and better than the primaries. SM-C1 counterbalances SM-1 by name ("closing threads is the goal, locking them is not"), SM-C2 counterbalances SM-4, and SM-C4 does something rare: it puts a measurement on the PRD's own load-bearing assumption, watching lazy materialisation "to learn whether it actually bounds growth the way §6.3 assumes, before a customer discovers it does not." That is a counter-metric doing real work rather than decorating a template.

Two problems, one of which the PRD half-noticed. §10 correctly frames the telemetry constraint and commits to "direct queries" on a reference deployment. But SM-5 (median position, in the Directory's default order, of the Thread a participant opens) and SM-6 (share of Directory sessions that use search) are not answerable by any database query — they require per-session client event instrumentation, which appears in no FR, no NFR and no line of §9.1. The PRD identified the measurement problem and then wrote two metrics that its own stated method cannot produce. SM-1, SM-2, SM-4 and SM-C4 are all genuinely query-answerable, so this is a specific defect in two metrics, not a general failure.

Separately, the MVP takes all thirty-nine FRs. §9.2 defers real things, but among what is in scope there is no phasing, no ranking, and no statement of what would be cut under pressure — on a fork with a named merge liability, a blocked verification path for two FRs, and one Open Question that blocks two more. Answering all ten issue items at once is scope acceptance; it is not prioritisation, and §14's completeness table makes the absence look like thoroughness.

### Findings

- **high** SM-5 and SM-6 cannot be measured by the method §10 commits to (§10 Secondary) — both need session-level client events (which Thread was opened and at what list position; whether search was used in a session). §10 says primary metrics are "measured on a consenting reference deployment through direct queries", nothing in §9.1 puts instrumentation in scope, and §6.2 commits to not shipping telemetry off customer servers. As written these two are unfalsifiable, which also silently disarms SM-5's role as the check on FR-12's ordering. *Fix:* either add local (non-exfiltrating) event logging on the reference deployment to §9.1 as an explicit deliverable, or replace SM-5/SM-6 with query-answerable proxies — e.g. rank-of-opened-Thread derived from the Thread Read Marker's update order, and search usage from server-side request counts on the search endpoint.
- **medium** MVP has no internal phasing or cut order (§9.1) — thirty-nine FRs, ten issue items, one release, on a fork §6.1 calls a compounding merge cost. Nothing states which of the nine features could ship second, even though FR-31/FR-35 are blocked on Q8 and FR-26/FR-27 are blocked on Q1. *Fix:* mark a phase boundary — lifecycle plus authority plus Directory plus notification-payload fix is a coherent first cut; labels and per-Thread unread are the two heaviest and the two with live open questions.
- **medium** SM-2 rewards the volume the Vision treats as the symptom, with no counter (§10 Primary, §1) — "Thread creation rate … Target: no decline, and ideally growth." The Vision's own framing is that a room with two hundred threads is a room where nobody uses threads. Growth in creation with no matching growth in closure is the failure mode returning, and no counter-metric pairs with SM-2. *Fix:* express SM-2 as creation rate against closure rate, or pair it with a counter on net live-thread count per Conversation.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The method here is better than most launch-grade PRDs. Every FR carries a **Consequences (testable)** block, and the good ones are genuinely unforgiving: FR-5 enumerates six independent write paths as six separate consequences so "a missed path fails a test, not a customer"; FR-27 states five cases including both directions of the main-chat interaction and the leave-and-rejoin case; FR-30 states four cases covering all combinations of main-chat and thread unread; FR-8 tests authority per request and specifies that demotion takes effect "immediately on the next request rather than at session end." An engineer could write integration tests from most of this document without asking a question.

Four defects cut against it, and one is serious enough to name first. FR-26's last consequence says a participant who has never opened a Thread "gets a count derived from their Conversation read marker." FR-27's second consequence says "With unread messages in Thread A, reading the Conversation's main chat to its end leaves A's count unchanged." For a never-opened Thread, these cannot both hold — advancing the Conversation marker by reading the main chat necessarily changes the derived count. Open Question 1 names this exact collision and defers it, and addendum §4.4 argues for option 3. But deferral does not help the story workflow: a story generated from FR-27 produces a test that fails, and a story generated from FR-26 produces one that passes, and both cite the PRD correctly.

Second, there is no time bound anywhere in a document whose first NFR is "Scale is the point." §5 gives a scale condition (a thousand Threads, two hundred participants) and no latency figure. FR-16's "The first page returns without the participant waiting on the full Thread count for the Conversation" describes an implementation property, not a verifiable one. SM-C3's "Must not regress" has no baseline, because the surface it measures does not exist yet. Third, three consequences depend on display bounds the PRD does not set (FR-15's "bounded number" of labels, FR-28's "above a display bound"). Fourth, two consequences are stated as effects on a reader's understanding rather than as verifiable conditions — and one of them is load-bearing: FR-7's "The Closed indicator **conveys** that the Thread can still be posted in and that posting reopens it" is the entire mitigation for a naming risk the requester knowingly accepted, and it is the least testable line in the document.

### Findings

- **critical** FR-26 and FR-27 specify contradictory behaviour for never-opened Threads (§4.7 FR-26 final consequence, FR-27 second consequence, §13 Q1) — the fallback to the Conversation read marker and the absolute "reading the main chat does not clear it in any Thread" are incompatible. Q1 acknowledges the collision and defers it to architecture, but leaves both consequences standing as written, so downstream story creation will generate mutually failing acceptance criteria from the same feature. *Fix:* scope FR-27's consequence to Threads that have a Thread Read Marker, and add an explicit consequence for the never-opened case that states the chosen resolution (or states that the case is undefined pending Q1 and must not be tested). The behaviour can stay open; the FR text cannot.
- **high** No latency or page-size bound anywhere, in a PRD whose first NFR is scale (§5, FR-16, SM-C3) — "Scale is the point" gives a data condition and no time condition. FR-16 has no page size and no response-time bound; SM-C3 says "must not regress" against a baseline that does not exist. §12's third risk ("Unread counts turn into a per-Thread query … the Directory being slow at exactly the moment it matters") has a mitigation that measures nothing absolute. *Fix:* put a number in §5 — e.g. Directory first page ≤ N rows returning within X ms at p50 and Y ms at p95 in a Conversation of a thousand Threads — and let SM-C3 measure against it. Without one, "the Directory is slow" is not a failable condition at any point in the chain.
- **medium** Display bounds referenced but never set (FR-15 fourth consequence, FR-28 fifth consequence) — "a bounded number" of labels per row and a count "above a display bound" both require values an engineer cannot invent consistently across two surfaces. *Fix:* state both (labels shown per row; unread cap, conventionally 99+).
- **medium** Two consequences specify comprehension rather than a verifiable condition (FR-7 final consequence, FR-13 second consequence) — "The Closed indicator conveys that the Thread can still be posted in" and "the filter makes clear that Threads are being hidden rather than absent." FR-7's is the accepted mitigation for the confirmed "Closed" collision (§4.1 Notes), so it carries the most weight and offers the least to test. *Fix:* specify the artefact instead of the effect — e.g. the Closed indicator carries a tooltip or inline string stating that posting reopens the Thread, and the state filter shows the hidden count ("22 Ongoing · 158 hidden").

## Scope honesty — adequate

Omissions are mostly explicit and several are sharp. §8's eight non-goals do real work — "Not a task tracker" pre-empts the most likely misreading of Thread State ("A Closed Thread means the talking stopped, not that work finished"), "Not private organisation" forecloses personal pins before anyone asks, and "Not threads-of-threads" closes a question the glossary would otherwise leave open. §9.2 separates deferral from refusal explicitly, and one entry defers something the PRD itself specified: "Reactions in Locked Threads — FR-5 refuses them on an assumption that may not survive review." De-scoping is proposed, not performed silently. The `[NOTE FOR PM]` on auto-close is placed at a real tension and marked "Load-bearing", not at a safe checkpoint.

The assumption discipline holds up mechanically: twenty-one inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags round-trip into twenty §16 entries (one entry legitimately covers FR-22's two label bounds), with no orphans in either direction. Open-items density is high in absolute terms — eight Open Questions, twenty-one assumptions, two PM notes against a fully in-scope thirty-nine-FR MVP — but each is owned and only three are load-bearing, so the density is informative rather than evasive.

One omission is unnamed and it is not small. Thread identity is the root comment id, and this build ships per-conversation message expiration (`message-expiration` in `CONDITIONAL_FEATURES`, `lib/BackgroundJob/ExpireChatMessages.php`) as well as ordinary message deletion. FR-34's fourth consequence assumes deleted Threads exist ("Following one for a Thread since deleted"), and FR-5 refuses deletion *inside* a Locked Thread — but nothing anywhere says what happens to Thread State, Pinned Threads, Thread Labels or Thread Read Markers when a Thread's own root message is deleted or expires. That is not a corner: a customer with expiration enabled will meet it on a schedule.

Separately, §14's row 8 is the one place the traceability table lets the reader assume more than the PRD delivers.

### Findings

- **high** Thread lifecycle under root-message deletion and message expiration is unaddressed (§3 Thread Root Message, §4.1, FR-34, §8, §9.2) — the glossary states "a Thread cannot exist without it", and Talk ships both message deletion and per-conversation expiration, yet no FR, non-goal or open question covers what becomes of State, pins, labels and read markers when the root goes. FR-34 assumes the deleted-Thread case exists without anything having created it. *Fix:* add an FR (or an explicit `[NON-GOAL for MVP]`) stating the behaviour — most likely that Thread rows and their new attributes are removed with the root, and that the Directory and Followed Thread List drop the row — and confirm expiration is covered, not just manual deletion.
- **medium** §14 row 8 reads as delivered when the behaviour the issue asked for is deferred (§14 row 8, §9.2, §11) — item 8 is "Push notification click opens the thread", mapped to FR-32/33/34 with the note "Server-side defect, not a client gap — §4.8." True, and it reframes rather than qualifies: no shipped client reads the corrected payload (§9.2), FR-34's consequences are web-only, and §11 states the payload cannot be verified onto a device at all because the delivery hop is blocked on credentials Clavis does not have. Rows 4, 9 and 10 all carry qualifying notes; the one row where the qualification matters most does not. *Fix:* change the note to say the server payload lands here and the push-click behaviour lands with the mobile epic, unverifiable on-device until the APNs/FCM path exists.

## Downstream usability — strong

This PRD is built to be consumed. The glossary fixes fifteen terms and does something genuinely useful for brownfield work: each entry states its provenance — "Already exists upstream; this PRD extends it", "New in this PRD", "Pre-existing; named here only to fix the word." An architecture or story workflow can tell extension from invention without reading the addendum. FR IDs run 1–39 contiguous and unique; UJ-1…UJ-5 and SM-1…SM-6 plus SM-C1…SM-C4 are complete; every cross-reference I followed resolves, including the addendum's section pointers (§10 for comparables, §4.4 for the unread fallback options) and the Open Question back-references from §16 items 6 and 14. The addendum/PRD split is disciplined in the right direction — the PRD says what, the addendum says where, and the addendum's opening warning that line numbers "will drift — treat them as pointers to the right function, not as addresses" is exactly the right instruction to a downstream reader.

Sections stand alone. §4.7's preamble explains its own reasoning rather than referring back, §6.3 restates the cost it is accepting, and §14 lets a reader enter at the issue and get to requirements without reading §1–§13.

The weakness is a collision the glossary cannot resolve because the PRD does not know about it. This build already ships a user-facing pinning feature for messages — capability `pinned-messages`, UI strings "Pin message", "Unpin message", "Keep pinned for", plus a per-participant hide (`talk_attendees.hidden_pinned_id`) and an expiry (`pinUntil`). The PRD introduces "Pinned Thread" with none of those properties and says nothing about the two coexisting, even though a Thread Root Message is a message and can therefore be pinned in the older sense while its Thread is pinned in the new one. Similarly, "Thread Label" arrives alongside a shipped `conversation-tags` feature whose interface says "New tag", "Rename tag", "Delete tag". Downstream will have to invent the disambiguation.

### Findings

- **medium** "Pinned Thread" collides with the shipped `pinned-messages` feature and the interaction is unspecified (§3 glossary, FR-20, FR-21) — `lib/Capabilities.php:131` declares `pinned-messages`; the UI offers "Pin message"/"Unpin message"/"Keep pinned for"; `talk_attendees.hidden_pinned_id` gives each participant a private hide. FR-20 defines a second, differently-scoped pin ("not a private bookmark", no expiry) on the object whose root is a pinnable message, and neither FR-20 nor FR-21 nor the glossary says whether the two are independent, whether pinning a Thread pins its root, or how the Directory treats a Thread whose root is message-pinned. §8's "Not private organisation … per-participant ordering are not in this product" is also stated without noticing that the existing pin already has a per-participant hide. *Fix:* add a glossary sentence distinguishing Pinned Thread from a pinned message, and one FR-20 consequence stating the two are independent (or specifying the coupling).
- **low** The PRD violates its own synonym rule 47 times (§3 Conversation, §1, §2, §10, §12) — the glossary fixes "Conversation" and states "Introducing a synonym anywhere is a discipline violation", then the Vision, JTBDs, journeys, SM-3 and §12 use "room" throughout. Harmless to a human, but the glossary told downstream workflows to treat it as an error. *Fix:* either use Conversation in prose or amend the glossary entry to permit "room" in narrative sections and forbid it in FRs, UJ titles and SM definitions.
- **low** "thread header" is a load-bearing surface used six times and never glossed (FR-7, FR-37, UJ-2) — "Thread Directory" and "Followed Thread List" both got glossary entries; the third surface did not, though FR-37's consequences depend on what it is. *Fix:* add a one-line glossary entry.

## Shape fit — adequate

The shape is right. This is a brownfield, chain-top, multi-stakeholder capability spec, and it is written as one: UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing rather than ornamental, §7 gives the API-compatibility constraint that a chain-top PRD for a public OCS surface must give, §6.1 gives the fork constraint, and the implementation detail is pushed to a companion rather than smuggled into FRs. Neither over- nor under-formalised.

Where I checked the existing-code claims, they are accurate and load-bearing. The notification defect in §4.8 and addendum §2 is exactly as described — `lib/Notification/Notifier.php:604-610` sets the link outside any push guard while `:633-642` sets the object inside `!isPreparingPushNotification()`, and the second `setObject` does overwrite the first, destroying the message id four lines below a comment promising to forward it. The three dead columns claim in addendum §4.6 holds: `has_unread_threads` and siblings are declared on `Attendee`, hydrated in `AttendeeMapper`, selected in `SelectHelper`, and have no getter or setter call anywhere in `lib/` or `src/`. `Capabilities.php:130` declares `threads` in `FEATURES` as stated. This is careful reconnaissance and the PRD's confidence in it is earned.

The blind spot is on adjacent features rather than the thread machinery, and it distorts two conclusions. Addendum §5.1 opens "Nothing comparable exists; there is no conversation-level label or tag mechanism in Talk to extend." This build ships `lib/Service/ConversationTagService.php` with `MAX_TAG_IDS_PER_CONVERSATION = 20`, `MAX_CUSTOM_TAGS_PER_USER = 100`, `MAX_TAG_NAME_LENGTH`, dedicated `InvalidTagNameException` / `TagLimitExceededException` / `TagNameAlreadyInUseException`, a `talk_conversation_tags` table with a uniqueness index, `ConversationTagMapper::` cleanup on tag deletion, and a full rename/delete/reorder UI. It is per-user and applies to Conversations rather than Threads, so it is not the same feature — but it is the closest precedent in the codebase for every problem FR-22 through FR-25 solve, including the name normalisation addendum §5.1 flags as needing a decision and the label administration §9.2 defers to v2 as unbuilt.

The same blindness runs through the naming analysis. Addendum §10.6 rules out "tag" on the strength of Microsoft Teams and rules out "archive" on the strength of Slack and Discord. Both conclusions are right, and both miss the decisive local evidence: this product's own interface already says "New tag" / "Rename tag" / "Delete tag" for conversation grouping and "Archived conversations" / "Archive conversation" in the left sidebar (`archived-conversations-v2`). A survey that reaches four competitors and not the product under change produced correct answers for two words and, because "pin" was never checked, missed the third collision entirely.

### Findings

- **high** Addendum §5.1's premise is false: a tag mechanism ships in this build (addendum §5.1, §5.4; PRD §4.6, §9.2) — `lib/Service/ConversationTagService.php`, `lib/Model/ConversationTag.php`, `lib/Model/ConversationTagMapper.php`, `talk_conversation_tags` (created in `lib/Migration/Version24000Date20260313120000.php`), capability `conversation-tags`, and a `src/stores/conversationTags.ts` + `ConversationTagHeader.vue` UI. It already solves per-scope name uniqueness, a 20-item cap, trim/validation errors, and cascade cleanup on delete — and it already implements the rename/delete-everywhere surface §9.2 lists as a v2 deferral. FR-22's 20-per-Conversation bound is attributed to "Discord's cap" when the same number is already a constant in this codebase for a sibling concept. *Fix:* correct §5.1, point the architecture workflow at `ConversationTagService` as the pattern to mirror (or deliberately diverge from) for label identity, normalisation and limits, and revisit whether §9.2's label-administration deferral is as expensive as it was assumed to be.
- **high** The naming analysis surveys four competitors and not the product being changed (addendum §10.6, PRD §4.1 Notes) — "tag" is already a user-facing word here (`conversation-tags`; "New tag", "Rename tag", "Tag name"), "archive" is already a user-facing word here (`archived-conversations-v2`; "Archive conversation", "Archived conversations"), and "pin" — never examined — is already a user-facing word here (`pinned-messages`; "Pin message", "Keep pinned for"). The Teams and Slack arguments are sound but secondary: a Clavis Talk user does not have to come from another product to be confused. Because "pin" was not checked, the collision the PRD actually introduces went unnoticed. *Fix:* add an in-product vocabulary check to §10.6 — it strengthens the "label" and anti-"archive" conclusions and forces the Pinned Thread disambiguation named under Downstream usability.
- **medium** Addendum §5.2 treats thread pinning as green-field and ignores the shipped pin implementation (addendum §5.2) — it proposes "a boolean or a nullable ordering value on `talk_threads`" plus sort changes, with no reference to the existing message-pin design, which already solved ordering (`pinnedAt` sort in `ChatController.php:2069,2128`), per-user suppression (`hidden_pinned_id`), and expiry (`pinUntil`). Either those are precedents to reuse or their absence from FR-20 is a divergence worth declaring. *Fix:* add the comparison, and note explicitly that Pinned Thread deliberately has no expiry and no per-participant hide, so the difference is a decision rather than an oversight.

## Mechanical notes

- **Assumptions Index round-trip: clean.** Twenty-one real inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (excluding the convention mention in §0 and the §16 header) map to twenty index entries, with item 10 legitimately covering FR-22's two bounds. No orphans in either direction. Index entries carry their own severity commentary (item 1 "Highest-leverage item in this list", item 6 "Weakest assumption in the document"), which is more than the format requires and worth keeping.
- **ID continuity: clean.** FR-1…FR-39 contiguous and unique; UJ-1…UJ-5, SM-1…SM-6, SM-C1…SM-C4 complete. Open Question renumbering after Q3's resolution left no stale references — FR-5's assumption→Q2, §16 item 6→Q2, §16 item 14→Q1 and §14 row 10→Q8 all land correctly.
- **One traceability omission:** FR-3 appears in no §14 row (see Decision-readiness). Every other FR is claimed by at least one row.
- **Glossary drift:** "room" for Conversation, 47 occurrences (see Downstream usability). Also "thread" and "threads" lowercase throughout §1 and §12 where the glossary term is "Thread" — same class, same fix.
- **`[NON-GOAL for MVP]` callout form is used zero times.** §8 and §9.2 carry the load and mostly carry it well, so this is not a gap in itself — but the deletion/expiration omission is precisely where an inline callout would have caught the silent assumption.
- **FR-5's reaction consequence sits on a conditional capability.** `reactions` is declared in `CONDITIONAL_FEATURES`, so a server can have reactions disabled entirely; FR-5's "Reacting to a message in a Locked Thread is refused" needs a no-op branch for that case. Minor, but it is the kind of thing that surfaces as a failing test rather than a design conversation.
- **Required sections: all present** for a launch-grade brownfield chain-top PRD — Vision, Target User with JTBD and non-users, Glossary, Features with FRs, NFRs, Constraints, API compatibility, Non-Goals, MVP scope, Success Metrics with counter-metrics, Dependencies, Risks, Open Questions, Traceability, Assumptions Index, companion documents.
